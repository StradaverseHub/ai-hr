| D-022 | DECISION FOR OWNER | No notifications to the referrer on stage changes or rejection (EV-038). The referrer sees a coarse status on My referrals instead. | — | Owner decides whether referrers are notified, and of what. |
| D-023 | IMPLEMENTATION DECISION | A refused referral shows the referrer one generic message. The reference says "already referred internally" or "similar application in the system", which tells an employee that a named person has applied. The specific reason is in the audit event for HR. Residual risk: a refusal on a visible job still implies the person applied or was recently referred, so refused attempts count toward the daily limit (D-024) and each one is audited. Hiding it fully would mean accepting duplicates silently and flagging them to HR. | — | Owner prefers the specific messages, or silent acceptance with an HR flag. |
| D-024 | IMPLEMENTATION DECISION | At most 20 referral attempts (accepted or refused) per referrer per Kuala Lumpur day. The reference shows no limit. | — | Owner sets a different limit. |
| D-025 | IMPLEMENTATION DECISION | One plain careers page per company in the AI HR look (address, display name, headline, introduction, privacy notice). The reference has multiple designed career websites with a page builder, posters, QR codes and job-board posting (EV-039, EV-044…EV-046). | — | Owner asks for more sites, images or job-board posting. |
| D-026 | DECISION FOR OWNER | No applicant accounts and no e-mail verification code. The reference makes applicants sign in with an e-mail code, which creates an account (EV-043). Sending codes to any address on request needs an outbound-mail and abuse design, and AI HR has no identity type for applicants. Consequence: the e-mail is unverified, applicants cannot see their status or withdraw online, and a public submission is never merged into an existing candidate. | — | Owner decides whether applicants need accounts or e-mail verification. |
| D-027 | IMPLEMENTATION DECISION | Every well-formed submission gets the same thank-you page, including repeated applications (not stored) and honeypot hits; limits of 5 submissions per hour per client address and 300 accepted per company per Kuala Lumpur day. The reference behaviour for repeats on its public site was not observed. A visitor who knows someone's e-mail cannot learn whether they applied; a genuine applicant re-applying inside the window is not told it was not stored. The per-address limit needs the installation's web server to pass each visitor's real address (the same verified ingress step as sign-in protection, `AIHR_TRUST_REQUEST_SOURCE`); until that step is done the form says online applications are unavailable (the pages stay viewable), rather than letting one visitor use up a shared limit for everyone. | — | Owner changes the limits or wants repeats shown; installation completes the ingress step. |

| D-029 | DECISION FOR OWNER | No confirmation e-mail to the applicant and no notification to HR on a new careers application (the same position as D-022). | — | Owner decides on messages once the messaging area is in scope. |
