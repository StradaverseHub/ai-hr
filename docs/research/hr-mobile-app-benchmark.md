# Win the clock-in, not the feature list

Across 30-odd HR, payroll and time-and-attendance apps, the mobile product that earns or loses ratings is **the clock-in button**. Scope is near-identical everywhere (leave, claims, payslips, clocking, an approvals inbox). Store complaints are dominated by location fixes that take minutes, face checks that fail, forced logouts and "offline" errors at shift start. In Malaysia a failed punch becomes a lateness penalty, so the stakes are high. Anti-spoofing across the market stops at **GPS geofence plus a selfie**. Only a handful of vendors document mock-location blocking, a couple document device binding, and **none documents App Attest or Play Integrity, or a trustworthy clock for offline punches**. Our draft ADR-0088 is therefore ahead of the market on integrity and correct on its main calls: native Expo, a thin versioned API over `core/`, PKCE sign-in, content-free push, and flag-don't-refuse. But the platform research **contradicts four of its technical assumptions**. The OS mock-location flags can only prove a location is fake. They can never prove it is genuine, and on iOS they miss third-party spoofers. Neither attestation service can attest an event while offline. iOS has no documented sleep-inclusive public clock matching the ADR's "monotonic elapsed time". And the ADR treats a selfie and face matching as the same PDPA question when they are not. The ranked changes in the final section fix those, add the reliability features the reviews demand, and pull store-compliance work forward from Phase 4.

## Ratings split on hourly-worker reliability, and Malaysian apps sit a full star lower

iOS ratings say little: five of six enterprise suites sit at 4.6–4.7 on the App Store. Google Play separates them. **ADP 4.6, Dayforce 4.5, Workday 4.2, UKG Pro 4.1, SAP 3.1, Oracle 2.5** ([Play: ADP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adpmobile.android); [Play: SAP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.successfactors.successfactors); [Play: Oracle](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.oracle.cloud.hcm.mobile)). The two leaders are the apps built around an hourly worker's daily jobs: clock, schedule, pay. The laggards are the most web-derived. Oracle's phone app shows only "mobile responsive" pages of the web product ([Oracle docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/human-resources/faucf/oracle-fusion-applications-mobile-app.html)). That is independent support for ADR-0088's rejection of a Capacitor wrapper. Deskless-workforce specialists post 4.8–4.9 on iOS (Connecteam 4.9 from 59K ratings, When I Work 4.8, Deputy 4.8), but their kiosk apps rate far lower. Time Clock by Homebase has 2.3 ([App Store: Connecteam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/connecteam-team-management-app/id1121613912); [App Store: Time Clock by Homebase](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/time-clock-by-homebase/id966274715)).

The Malaysian field is weaker and more attendance-driven. The most-installed app, Info-Tech HRMS, has **1M+ installs at 3.79 on Play and 3.82 on the MY App Store**. SQL HRMS scores 4.59 on Play but 3.03 on iOS, AutoCount AC Payroll 3.97 / 2.32, Worksy 3.77 / 3.40 and BrioHR 3.81 / 2.73 ([Play: Info-Tech](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.infotech.mobileattendancenew); [App Store MY: AC Payroll](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/ac-payroll/id1464430538); [App Store MY: BrioHR](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/briohr/id1484448446)). The researchers pulled Info-Tech's 200 newest text reviews on Play: **131 were 1–2 stars, and 30 of those mention face or camera failures**. Reviewers write that face recognition "make me late to clockin" ([Play: Info-Tech](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.infotech.mobileattendancenew)). Other reviews report location fixes that take "3 4 minutes" on AutoCount and 5–10 minutes on BrioHR. A Worksy user counted sync failures ("15 times in 2024, 33 times in 2025, 36 times in 2026"). SQL HRMS gets "Server down all the time. Can't clock in on time almost everyday" ([Play: AC Payroll](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.autocountsoft.payroll); [Play: Worksy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.worksy); [Play: SQL HRMS](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=my.com.sql.app.hrms)).

The same failure classes recur in every segment. Location mis-reads: Dayforce "20 miles away", ADP "NOT on the job location". Collapsed error messages: ADP's Quick Clock says "you are offline" for any failure. Clocking blocked during maintenance windows: Dayforce overnight, Sprout during work hours ([Play: Dayforce](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dayforce.mobile); [Play: ADP](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adpmobile.android); [App Store PH: Sprout](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sprout-hr/id1434660055)). The second-largest class is login friction. "Stay signed in" does nothing (Workday). A failed fingerprint forces a full login plus a code on return from break (UKG). Swingvy and HReasily users must re-login on every open ([Play: Workday](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.workday.workdroidapp); [Play: UKG Pro](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ukg.oneapp); [Play: HReasily](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hreasily.sg.employee)). **OBSERVATION:** these review excerpts are listing-page samples, not statistical distributions. The ranking of themes is qualitative, but it is consistent across all four research streams.

## Every vendor ships the same scope; Malaysia adds EA forms and Malay-speaking users

**FACT:** all six enterprise suites put pay statements, leave, clock or timesheet, directory and a manager approvals inbox on mobile. Each ships **one store app for employees and managers**, gated by role and tenant, with separate apps only for shared-device kiosks and adjacent products ([Play: Workday](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.workday.workdroidapp); [SAP Mobile Feature Matrix](https://help.sap.com/doc/aa6156e451a145f9b66d8cf6625c5a49/latest/en-US/SF_MOB_Feature_Matrix.pdf); [Play: Workday Time Kiosk](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.workday.timekiosk)). The SMB platforms follow the same split: Rippling Timeclock, Employment Hero Time Clock and Zoho People Kiosk sit beside their main apps ([App Store: Rippling Timeclock](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rippling-timeclock/id1540084564); [Zoho Kiosk](https://www.zoho.com/people/kiosk.html)). Keeping payroll and admin on desktop is the norm. Only Gusto runs payroll from the phone, for owner-operated US micro-businesses ([App Store: Gusto](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gusto-mobile/id1478804271)). SAP goes the other way and puts **Terminate** and deductions into mobile manager self-service ([SAP Mobile Feature Matrix](https://help.sap.com/doc/aa6156e451a145f9b66d8cf6625c5a49/latest/en-US/SF_MOB_Feature_Matrix.pdf)). ADR-0088's scope sits squarely in the consensus.

Malaysia adds three things the ADR does not yet name. First, **Borang EA download on mobile** is explicitly listed by Kakitangan, SQL HRMS ("Unlimited retrieval of EA form") and AutoCount ([Play: Kakitangan](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kakitangan.main); [Play: SQL HRMS](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=my.com.sql.app.hrms); [Play: AC Payroll](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.autocountsoft.payroll)). Second, **medical-certificate upload on sick leave** is a headline feature ("Instant upload of medical certificate", AutoCount), and so is half-day or hourly leave. Third, **sharing a payslip out of the app** is a real job: Info-Tech lets employees share a password-protected payslip "via e-mail or WhatsApp", typically for a bank loan ([Play: Info-Tech](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.infotech.mobileattendancenew)). One further complaint bears directly on our identity model. Info-Tech reviewers report that **payslip access disappears on termination** and former employees cannot retrieve historical payslips ([Play: Info-Tech](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.infotech.mobileattendancenew)). ADR-0088 currently makes an offboarded user resolve to no principal. That is correct for the app, but it leaves the alumni-document job unowned.

Language is a cheap, credible differentiator. By App Store metadata, **most Malaysian HR apps ship English only**: SQL HRMS, AC Payroll, Kakitangan, BrioHR, Swingvy, Worksy. Info-Tech declares EN/MS/ZH, and only HR2eazy was found declaring Tamil ([App Store MY: Info-Tech](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/infotech-hrms/id1096521618)). Yet many low-star reviews of the biggest apps are written in Bahasa Malaysia. Our six catalogues (`en`, `ms`, `zh`, `ta`, `th`, `vi`) already cover all three local languages.

The convergent home screen is **task- or inbox-first with one-tap clock-in**: SAP Home, Personio Home plus Inbox, Deel's "tasks, pay date, balance", HiBob clock-in from home ([App Store: Personio](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/personio/id1470197212); [HiBob mobile brief](https://www.hibob.com/product-briefs/mobile-app/)). Home-screen widgets are an unmet request. Gusto and Rippling reviewers ask for them, and only Zoho and Dayforce ship one ([App Store: Zoho People](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/zoho-people-hr-management/id680525956); [App Store: Dayforce](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dayforce/id456073226)). Two warnings follow from redesign backlash (Zoho Sept 2026, Dayforce's calendar that caused missed shifts) and from ad-bundling complaints ("SWAG is not what we signed up for" at Employment Hero; pop-up ads at Gusto): **keep the daily clock and leave paths stable across releases, and carry no advertising in an employer-mandated app** ([App Store AU: Employment Hero](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/employment-hero-work/id1171253572?see-all=reviews)).

On AI, every enterprise suite now ships an assistant in its main mobile app: Workday Sana, SAP Joule, Dayforce AI Assistant, UKG Bryte. As documented, they are **Q&A plus deep links**, with narrow transactional actions gated by the same business-process security as the UI. Workday's assistant can request time off only under the Request Time Off permissions ([Workday Admin Guide](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/absence/time-off/set-up-time-off/qdb1617991549488.html)). Dayforce's pattern suits our ADR-0084 rule best. An answer carries **buttons that deep-link to the normal form**. A one-time disclaimer precedes first use. A thumbs-down can open an HR case ([Dayforce Help: AI Assistant](https://help.dayforce.com/r/documents/Dayforce-Mobile-App-Guide-Android/Dayforce-AI-Assistant)). In Malaysia the only AI shown on mobile is Kakitangan's human-reviewed receipt OCR, which extracts the **LHDN e-Invoice UUID** ([Kakitangan claims](https://www.kakitangan.com/solutions/claim)). ADR-0088's decision to keep AI on the web for now is consistent with a market where no Malaysian incumbent has a mobile "ask HR" assistant.

## Geofence plus selfie is the market's ceiling, and the OS flags do not raise it

Almost every vendor's integrity layer is a GPS geofence plus a photo at the punch. Malaysian vendors default to **a 200 m radius** ([Info-Tech](https://www.info-tech.com.sg/mobile-attendance-app); [Kakitangan Hadir guide](https://blog.kakitangan.com/hadir-user-guide/)). When I Work states that "geolocation is only accurate down to 100 meters" and enforces a 100 m minimum ([When I Work help](https://help.wheniwork.com/articles/restricting-where-users-can-clock-in-computer/)). The best-documented model is UKG Pro WFM's **Known Place**: a GPS radius **plus a GPS accuracy threshold**, Wi-Fi network or access point, and rotating QR codes. A punch is inside only if it is within the area and its accuracy value does not exceed the threshold. Fence changes are audit-reported ([UKG help: geofence setup](https://dcus14-hlp01.gss.mykronos.com/help/HCM/administration/Mobile_app/geofence_setup_and_usage_369167.html); [UKG geofencing guide](https://www.ukg.com/working-smarter-cafe/go-guide-geofencing-ukg-pro-wfm)). Deputy and Connecteam both show the employee their distance and fix accuracy before the punch ([Deputy help](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/4621616556047-How-do-I-clock-on-and-off-for-a-scheduled-shift); [Connecteam help](https://help.connecteam.com/en/articles/3597710-how-to-create-a-geofence)). Corroborating signals exist but are niche. Worksy and AutoCount offer workplace Wi-Fi clocking, and AutoCount also offers a Bluetooth beacon ([autocountsystem.com](https://autocountsystem.com/mobile-clock-in-ac-payroll-autocount-hrms/)). TimeTec offers beacon, NFC and Wi-Fi checkpoints ([TimeTec KB](https://support.timeteccloud.com/portal/en/kb/articles/timetec-ta-mobile-app-personal-clocking)). Rippling and Swingvy offer QR codes. Zoho adds two cheap anti-gaming rules: **check-out must come from the same source as check-in**, and **punches made through Siri or Shortcuts automation can be blocked** ([Zoho help](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/attendance-management/settings/articles/check-in-and-check-out-preferences)).

The market splits on **block versus flag**, and ADR-0088's flag-by-default sits with the more humane half. Oracle offers "Restrict" or "Record and report" ([Oracle 23A](https://www.oracle.com/webfolder/technetwork/tutorials/tutorial/cloud/r13/nfs/time-labor/releases/23A/23A-time-labor-nfs.htm)). Deel lets an off-site punch be "blocked or accepted with a warning to the approver" ([Deel blog](https://www.deel.com/blog/introducing-time-tracker/)). Swingvy routes out-of-fence punches to an approver ([Swingvy Time](https://www.swingvy.com/sg/products/time)). Connecteam offers "Clock out outside the fenced area anyway", which becomes an approval request ([Connecteam help](https://help.connecteam.com/en/articles/3597710-how-to-create-a-geofence)). Employment Hero, Homebase and Jibble hard-block. Documented mock-location handling is rare. Connecteam blocks the punch when "a third-party location app was identified" ([Connecteam help](https://help.connecteam.com/en/articles/8795895-can-employees-change-their-clock-in-location-with-a-third-party-app)). TimeTec logs the user out when Android mock location is on ([FingerTec](https://www.fingertectips.com/2016/12/timetec-ta-app-detects-mock-location-to.html)). Info-Tech claims it can "detect any activation of fake GPS", which is unverified. Reviewer evidence shows a mock app defeating Worksy and merely being *shown* in SQL HRMS ([Play: Worksy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.worksy); [Play: SQL HRMS](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=my.com.sql.app.hrms)). Device binding appears only as Jibble's "Device Lock" and TimeTec's resettable "Mobile ID" ([Jibble policy](https://www.jibble.io/help/configuring-your-time-tracking-policy); [FingerTec](https://www.fingertectips.com/2016/08/timetec-ta-having-problem-to-mobile.html)). SAP alone documents root/jailbreak detection, and it closes the app, which locks out some Android OEM ROMs ([SAP Mobile Security Guide](https://help.sap.com/doc/0a23f09204774bf5ad6e821cb65c1c9c/latest/en-US/SF_MOB_Security.pdf)). **No vendor in any segment documents App Attest or Play Integrity.** ADR-0088's attested registration would lead the market, not match it.

The platform research is what changes the ADR. **FACT:** Android `Location.isMock()` flags only locations from the framework's **test provider**, and LSPosed/Magisk modules exist specifically to hide it ([Android reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/location/Location); [HideMockLocation](https://github.com/auag0/HideMockLocation)). **FACT:** iOS `isSimulatedBySoftware` describes Core Location's own simulation, such as Xcode GPX files. In August 2025 an Apple engineer answered a report that it never flags iTool AnyTo with "It is not possible to know how every single spoofing tool works" ([Apple Forums 797864](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/797864)). **FACT:** consumer iOS spoofers run from a desktop over USB **with no jailbreak** ([ios-location-spoofer](https://github.com/acheong08/ios-location-spoofer)). A genuine app on a genuine iPhone will therefore pass App Attest while reporting a spoofed location. **FACT:** App Attest proves "a valid instance of your app", and Apple itself warns that a modified OS "might bypass restrictions" ([Apple: Assessing fraud risk](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicecheck/assessing-fraud-risk)). Rooted Android devices with leaked keyboxes are reported to pass even `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` until Google revokes the keybox ([HackTricks](https://hacktricks.wiki/en/mobile-pentesting/android-app-pentesting/play-integrity-attestation-bypass.html)). The conclusion: **a mock flag of `true` is strong evidence of fraud, and a flag of `false` is no evidence of honesty.** iOS is the weaker anti-spoof platform, so on-site corroboration (a rotating QR code or site Wi-Fi) is the only signal that adds real cost on iPhone.

Face checks are where Malaysian vendors compete and where their users suffer most. BrioHR, HReasily, Info-Tech, AutoCount and Worksy all sell face clock-in. Only HReasily claims liveness ([Play: HReasily](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hreasily.sg.employee)), and no vendor publishes a false-reject rate or where face templates live. Globally, strong liveness sits on kiosks. Jibble's 3D spoof prevention works only in kiosk mode on Face ID-class devices ([Jibble help](https://www.jibble.io/help/face-spoofing-prevention)), and Deputy drops Face Unlock when its kiosk is offline ([Deputy Kiosk FAQ](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/4754374426511-Deputy-Kiosk-for-iPad-FAQs)). ADR-0088's exclusion of face matching is well founded. Its stated reason, though, is imprecise (see the contradictions section).

## Offline punches exist, but nobody publishes a trustworthy clock

Offline clocking is available at Jibble (on by default, clock in/out only, no breaks), Deputy Kiosk (up to **24 h**), Sprout One, AutoCount, Hubstaff, Clockify and legacy UKG ([Jibble offline](https://www.jibble.io/help/how-to-track-time-without-an-internet-connection); [App Store: Deputy Kiosk](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/deputy-kiosk-time-clock/id622403213); [Play: Sprout One](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ph.sprout.sproutnext)). Employment Hero's kiosk explicitly requires Wi-Fi, and Connecteam's help centre says the app cannot be used offline ([Connecteam help](https://help.connecteam.com/en/articles/9414361-can-the-connecteam-app-be-used-offline)). The best-documented trust rules come from Connecteam's clock API, built for physical clocks. It enforces a **12-hour look-back**, rejects future timestamps, requires the time to fall after the open shift's clock-in, and rejects **locked or approved days** ([Connecteam changelog](https://developer.connecteam.com/changelog/time-clock-clock-in-out-offline-mode-improved)). Jibble adds conflict quarantine. A colliding offline entry "is shown on the timesheet but it isn't calculated" until a manager resolves it, and automatic clock-out is disabled for offline punches ([Jibble offline](https://www.jibble.io/help/how-to-track-time-without-an-internet-connection)). Hubstaff claims idempotent sync and a "tamper-evident local cache", without disclosing the mechanism ([Hubstaff](https://hubstaff.com/time-tracking/accurate-offline-time-tracking)). **No vendor documents a monotonic-clock or boot-anchor technique.** ADR-0088's anchored design has no published peer, which is both an opportunity and a sign that it must be proven on devices.

Three platform facts bear directly on that design. First, **neither attestation service works at the moment of an offline event.** Apple's documented assertion flow requires the app to "first obtain a unique, one-time challenge from the server" ([Apple: Establishing your app's integrity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicecheck/establishing-your-app-s-integrity)). Play Integrity needs a Google round trip; Intune's documentation describes the user having to be online during that round trip ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/intune/app-management/protection/mam-faq)). Play Integrity also has a default quota of **10,000 requests per day across all installs**, and Google advises against caching verdicts ([Play Integrity overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/overview)). What does work offline is a **hardware-backed signing key attested while online**. `@expo/app-integrity` exposes Android hardware key attestation (`generateHardwareAttestedKeyAsync`, `getAttestationCertificateChainAsync`). The same docs label the library **alpha** with "frequently … breaking changes" ([Expo docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/app-integrity/)). **UNKNOWN:** whether an iOS App Attest assertion can be generated with no network over a locally built hash. The notes did not verify this, so it must be tested on a device before the design depends on it.

Second, **Android has the right clock and iOS does not document one.** Android's `SystemClock.elapsedRealtimeNanos()` includes deep sleep and is "guaranteed to be monotonic", but it "does not make sense… across boot cycles". GPS-provider fix times come from the satellite constellation, which gives independent corroboration ([Android SystemClock](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/SystemClock); [Android Location](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/location/Location)). iOS `ProcessInfo.systemUptime` is time the system "has been awake". It **excludes sleep**, so a pocketed phone under-counts elapsed time. It is also a **required-reason API** that must be declared in `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` ([Apple: systemUptime](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/processinfo/systemuptime)). **UNKNOWN:** the sleep semantics of `mach_continuous_time` could not be confirmed from Apple documentation in this research. Third, **a reboot destroys any monotonic anchor on both platforms.** The ADR's refusal rules do not cover that case.

## Login friction and lock-screen leaks are the quiet failure modes

On authentication, ADR-0088 already follows the standards. RFC 8252 says native apps "MUST use an external user-agent" with PKCE ([RFC 8252](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8252.txt)). WorkOS's official Expo example is a PKCE public client with "No client secrets in the app" and tokens in SecureStore ([workos/expo-authkit-example](https://github.com/workos/expo-authkit-example)). Two details are missing. RFC 9700 requires refresh-token **rotation or sender-constraining** for public clients, and WorkOS says tokens "may be rotated after use", so the app must always replace the stored token ([RFC 9700](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9700.txt); [WorkOS Sessions](https://workos.com/docs/authkit/sessions)). Also, iOS Keychain items may **persist across reinstall**, and SecureStore offers `*_THIS_DEVICE_ONLY` classes that never migrate through backup ([Expo SecureStore](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/securestore/)). The UX lesson from reviews is sharper than the security lesson. SAP's model is the mature one: a quick app passcode on foreground, biometrics that admins can allow "but this cannot be mandatory", and activation by company search, email link, QR code or MDM ([SAP Mobile Security Guide](https://help.sap.com/doc/0a23f09204774bf5ad6e821cb65c1c9c/latest/en-US/SF_MOB_Security.pdf)). Malaysian users repeatedly fail at the first step because they don't know which company or login applies. Payboy's "organiser code" and AutoCount users hunting for a sign-up button are examples ([Play: Payboy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.payboy.payboy_android)). Employment Hero's in-app **organisation picker** is the only documented multi-company switch, and it fits a person with several employments ([EH Help](https://help.employmenthero.com/hc/en-au/articles/360002611655-Switch-between-organisations)). MDM support is cheap only at the AppConfig level. UKG exposes `tenantInfo`, `isOrganizationalDevice` and `blockLocalAuthentication` keys ([UKG AppConfig keys](https://communityfiles.ukg.com/support/KOL/OnlineHelp-WorkforceDimensions/en-us/Content/Mobile/EMMPushKeys.htm)). Microsoft does not support React Native for the Intune App SDK, and Workday itself does not support Intune app-protection policies ([Microsoft Learn: MAM FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/intune/app-management/protection/mam-faq); [Microsoft Learn: Workday Mobile](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/saas-apps/workday-mobile-tutorial)).

On push, the ADR's content-free payload is **confirmed by policy**. Apple guideline 4.5.4 says pushes "should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Expo's relay holds payloads only in memory and queues, and native APNs/FCM tokens can bypass it entirely ([Expo push FAQ](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/faq/)). Android adds `VISIBILITY_PRIVATE` with a redacted `setPublicVersion`, though "the user always has ultimate control" ([Android notifications](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/build-notification)). SAP deliberately withholds push for compensation approvals ([SAP Mobile Feature Matrix](https://help.sap.com/doc/aa6156e451a145f9b66d8cf6625c5a49/latest/en-US/SF_MOB_Feature_Matrix.pdf)). No vendor documents approve/reject buttons on the notification itself. The unmet needs are **shift and forgotten-clock-out reminders** (ADP reviewers; Personio and Jibble ship them) and not re-prompting for permission after a user declines, a complaint against Kakitangan ([Play: Kakitangan](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kakitangan.main)).

On store policy and PDPA, the research tightens several points in ADR-0088. **FACT:** Google Play's Data safety form is required for closed, open and production tracks; only apps "exclusively active" on internal testing are exempt. Play Integrity use must be disclosed there ([Play Data safety](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469); [Play Integrity overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/overview)). **FACT:** background location needs a declaration form, a video and a prominent disclosure. Foreground location is Google's "preferred approach", and the policy points to the location button for one-time or transactional requests when targeting Android 17+ ([Play background location](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)). Features such as Workday's geofence entry reminders and Connecteam's auto clock-out on leaving the site both depend on background location, which is what drew Connecteam's "Always location" and battery complaints ([App Store: Connecteam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/connecteam-team-management-app/id1121613912)). SAP shows the precise-location trap: without **Precise location**, geofenced punches "will never be enabled" ([SAP Community](https://community.sap.com/t5/human-capital-management-blog-posts-by-sap/geofencing-capability-in-sap-successfactors-time-tracking/ba-p/14261861)). Account deletion is required only where accounts are created in or through the app. Google also exempts "permanently private and enterprise" apps. Reviewers still probe, so a deletion-request route is cheap insurance ([Play account deletion](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111); [Apple guidelines 5.1.1(v)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Managed-Play private apps cap at **1,000 organisations** and need a new package name to go public ([Google Play private apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/work/answer/6145139)). A single public listing is the right default. The Malaysian competitive bar is low. AutoCount, HReasily and Payboy declare **"No data collected"** despite GPS and face clock-in, Payboy declares data is not encrypted in transit, and no competitor privacy policy that was read mentions location or face data ([Play Data safety: AC Payroll](https://play.google.com/store/apps/datasafety?id=com.autocountsoft.payroll); [Play Data safety: Payboy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/datasafety?id=biz.payboy.payboy_android)).

## Where the research contradicts ADR-0088

**Mock-location flags (ADR §4, Risks).** The ADR lists flag reliability as UNKNOWN pending device tests, and treats "not flagged as simulated" as one of three conditions for `verified`. The research resolves the unknown in the negative. Apple has conceded that `isSimulatedBySoftware` does not catch third-party tools, and Android `isMock()` is hidden by widely available root modules ([Apple Forums 797864](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/797864); [HideMockLocation](https://github.com/auag0/HideMockLocation)). Device tests will only confirm the positive direction. The absence of a flag must contribute nothing to `verified`. **RISK:** as written, an iPhone with a USB desktop spoofer produces a `verified` punch from anywhere.

**Attestation offline (ADR §5).** "Signed with the attested key at the time of capture" cannot mean a Play Integrity token, which needs a Google round trip. It cannot mean a server-challenged App Attest assertion either, because there is no server to issue the challenge. ADR §4 also has "each mobile punch carries the device's attestation assertion". On Android that would mean one Play Integrity call per punch against a **10,000/day default quota** shared by every tenant ([Play Integrity overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/overview)). The signing key has to be a separate hardware-backed key, attested while online and bound to the registration.

**Monotonic clock on iOS (ADR §5).** The ADR assumes a monotonic elapsed time on both platforms. The documented iOS uptime API excludes sleep ([Apple: systemUptime](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/processinfo/systemuptime)), so the derived time would drift early by however long the phone slept. A sleep-inclusive iOS alternative is UNKNOWN until verified on devices. A reboot on either platform breaks the anchor, and the ADR has no state for that case.

**PDPA status of selfie/face data (ADR §4).** The ADR says "Face or selfie capture… is biometric personal data under PDPA." Under Act A1727, "biometric data" is data "resulting from technical processing" of physical characteristics, and it is now **sensitive personal data** requiring **explicit consent** under s.40(1)(a) ([Act A1727](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Act-A1727.pdf); [PDPA 2010](https://www.investmalaysia.gov.my/media/3x4fsqum/personal-data-protection-act-2010.pdf)). Face *matching* clearly falls in. A plain punch photo with no template extraction arguably does not. That is a **DECISION REQUIRED with Malaysian counsel**, not an engineering fact. **ASSUMPTION** (from the notes' reading of s.40(1)(b)(i)): the employment exception covers only obligations "conferred or imposed by law", and attendance verification is not one. If so, face matching would need withdrawable consent and a non-biometric fallback. Separately, biometric *app unlock* through the OS (Face ID via LocalAuthentication) gives the app no biometric data. The ADR should say so, or someone will later cite this exclusion against biometric unlock.

**Android location-permission policy (ADR silent).** The ADR never states foreground-only location. Any later "geofence reminder" or "auto clock-out" feature would trigger Play's background-location declaration and Apple's "Always" prompt ([Play background location](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)). The plan should commit to one-shot, user-initiated location now.

**Two smaller internal tensions.** First, ADR-0070 makes the **web path block** an out-of-fence punch (and log `attendance.geofence.blocked`), while ADR-0088 §4 reads as if a mobile out-of-fence punch is recorded and flagged instead. The same rule would then behave differently at two front doors: a competing source of truth. Second, ADR §6 persists only the punch queue. An offline clock screen still needs to know the employee's assigned sites, current in/out state, policy and time anchor. Without them it can neither show "you are clocked in" nor pre-check the fence. Phase 4 also places the privacy labels and Data safety form last, but any closed Play test track in Phases 1–3 already needs the form.

## Conclusion

The benchmark changes what "anti-spoofing" should mean for this app. The market treats it as a gate: block if outside the fence, block if the face does not match. That gate is exactly what produces the lateness complaints that dominate Malaysian reviews, while failing to stop a USB spoofer on an iPhone. The stronger position, which ADR-0088 is close to, is an **evidence ledger**: every punch and every failed attempt carries what the device could honestly attest. A deterministic adjudicator assigns a tier, and a human resolves the rest before payroll reads it. Two ideas carry this: asymmetric flags (a `true` mock flag counts, a `false` one counts for nothing) and attempt records that survive outages. Together they are the differentiator, more than attestation itself.

The second shift is sequencing. The expensive unknowns (offline App Attest assertions, a sleep-inclusive iOS clock, Play Integrity quota, `@expo/app-integrity` stability) all sit in Phase 3. Evidence capture, truthful errors, foreground-only location, the attempt log and the Data safety form are cheap and belong in Phase 1. Building them first means the Phase 3 adjudicator is calibrated on real Malaysian punches rather than on assumptions. It also means the first store build already beats most local competitors on honesty about the data it collects.
