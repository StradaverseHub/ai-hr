The same employee, the same month, the same payroll. On the left, how it prints today. On the right, how it prints after this change.

  • Basic salary RM 6,000.00
  • August 2026
  • Unpaid leave divisor: calendar days in the month (31)

⋯ is a real figure that is on the payslip but that this sketch does not draw. Because the absence comes off before EPF, SOCSO, EIS and PCB are worked out, all four change — by amounts that come from the statutory tables. We are not going to sketch a number we have not calculated. The salary they are worked out on is shown, because that one follows from the example.
“Other deductions” disappears here only because there was nothing else in it. If you also keyed other deductions that month, that line stays and shows what is left after the two named lines come out of it. When there are no unpaid and no absent days, neither line appears at all.

Example rows, to show the column in place. A day is counted as absent when it is a scheduled working day with no attendance record at all, or a day someone has explicitly marked Absent. Approved leave, public holidays and rest days are never counted.
Your roster wins. A day you rostered as a rest day is never counted, even when the company calendar calls it a working day — today the Attendance screens wrongly show that day in red as Absent, and we are fixing that at the same time so it can never cost anyone a day's pay.
This grid is only on runs that are still drafts, which is why the column will not appear on your 22 completed runs.

3What this does not change

It starts switched off. All 9 of your companies keep behaving exactly as they do now until you turn it on, in Payroll Policy, right beside your “Unpaid leave divisor” setting. It then applies to runs made from that point on.
Nothing already issued moves. Your 22 completed runs and 67 issued payslips print exactly what they print today. The two named lines appear on runs made after this is released.
No ABSENT pay code. A pay code would deduct a second time on top of the deduction already running, and it would be taken after EPF, SOCSO, EIS and PCB — so your contributions would be worked out on the full salary. That is why this is not drawn as a pay code.
