Why the figure reads 9.00 and not 10.00

Two limits apply to Special Leave, and an employee can only ever take the smaller of them. The entry shows that smaller number, because the larger one would promise a day FARAH cannot actually take.

The limitDays
Left in the shared pool (10.00 shared, 0.00 used)10.00
Her six types' own allowances, added up9.00
What she can actually take — the smaller of the two9.00

! Both of these are already enforced today. Nothing new is being enforced — we are only showing the answer instead of leaving it to be discovered at the point of refusal.

The two limits move independently. As soon as somebody uses one of the six types, the pool starts coming down and the group figure follows whichever of the two is lower at that moment. On a group whose member types carry more allowance between them than the shared limit, the pool is the one that binds and the figure would read the pool instead.

Opening the Special Leave entry shows both halves, so the figure is never a mystery.

Why the member types are gathered in rather than listed as well. Showing an employee the same ten days twice on one screen — once as a group and once as six rows — is the most reliable way a balance screen gets reported back as wrong. If you would rather have both, that is a fair call to make, and the plan says how to ask for it.
