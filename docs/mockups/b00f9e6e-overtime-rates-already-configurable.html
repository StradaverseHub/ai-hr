The RM 4,000 wage ceiling is the Employment Act s.60A overtime-eligibility limit — a legal threshold, not a free operational rate. Change it only if the law changes.

The 1.5× workday rate and the base-day pay (½/1-day rest-day, 2 days for a public holiday) are fixed by the Employment Act. The rest-day and public-holiday excess-hours rates default to the legal 2× / 3× and can be adjusted here.

These two boxes are your “Overtime 2.0” and “Overtime 3.0”. On a rest day, hours beyond a normal day’s hours are paid at two times the person’s own hourly rate; on a public holiday, three times. The hourly rate is worked out from each person’s own monthly wage, so a manager and a general worker are never paid the same amount — which is exactly what a flat ringgit-per-hour pay item could never do. All 22 of your completed payroll runs and the 67 payslips they produced were worked out this way.

A salary-following overtime item is possible, but not drawn. If you want a standing overtime item for people above the RM 4,000 ceiling that follows each of their own salaries, the Formula Builder can express it — basic salary ÷ your working days ÷ your working hours × the multiplier × the hours claimed. We would need the two divisors your firm uses, so it is not part of this plan. Reject and say so, and we build it.
