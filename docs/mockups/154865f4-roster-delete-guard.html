There is no exposure today. The top row is complete: every way into the system today goes through a screen that already refuses all three actions to anyone who is not an HR administrator. We checked every route that removes a shift - the roster grid, the multi-day clear, and the roster upload - and all of them ask that question first.

Why it is still worth closing. The second lock is what makes the first one safe to be wrong about. Today, anyone adding a new way to put shifts in - a new screen, an import, a bulk tool - inherits the protection for add and change without having to think about it, and silently does not inherit it for remove. That is the kind of gap nobody notices until it is used.

It is also a sentence we have been writing that is not true. We have said the store underneath decides who may change the roster. It decides that for two of the three actions.

How the "measured" cells were established: we acted as a real supervisor against a real direct report, and as an employee against their own day, using the same restricted identity the running system uses - not an administrator, and not a shortcut. Every attempt to add or change a future day was refused. Every attempt to remove one succeeded. The control case, removing a day the actor is not allowed to see, quietly did nothing, which is the expected behaviour. All of it ran inside a transaction that was rolled back, so no record was changed.

Nobody gains or loses a permission, no figure changes, and no screen changes. An HR administrator's roster works exactly as it does today.
