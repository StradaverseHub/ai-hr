Q, C, and the three figures that were sharing two names

Employee A (A00001): two children — one disabled and in higher education, one under 18. You were right, and our two earlier answers were wrong. Nobody's tax changes; what changes is the figures our documents print to describe it.

LHDN, monthly-tax specification 2026, printed page 10 “Q — Deduction for qualifying children. C — Number of qualifying children.” The formula subtracts Q × C. Printed page 27: “Relief to be given as if the employee has this number of children” — disabled child in higher education: 8; child under 18: 1.

One wrinkle we will not tidy away

Where a child's relief is shared 50/50 with the other parent, C comes out as a half — 4.5, for example. LHDN's table has no half child, because a shared claim is settled at the annual assessment rather than monthly. We will print the figure we actually used. Rounding it to 4 or 5 would make it stop multiplying to the relief printed beside it, and a slip whose own arithmetic does not check out is worse than an unusual number on it.
