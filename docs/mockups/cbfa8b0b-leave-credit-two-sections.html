Section B’s arithmetic already exists. The thresholds you describe — under four hours nothing, four to eight a half day, eight or more a full day, capped at one, rounded down — were built a fortnight ago, along with the per-company switch, the break deduction and the grace period. What is new here is the screen: one request holding both kinds of claim, and letting the employee name the hours they are claiming.

NOT A SCREEN CHANGE4. Splitting an evening between credit and overtime

You ask that 18:00–22:00 can be a leave credit while 22:00–00:00 is still paid as overtime:

Shift  09:00–18:00
18:00–22:00  credit
22:00–00:00  paid OT
Today the system cannot do that, and it is not because nobody wrote the screen. A leave credit currently cancels that day’s overtime pay for the whole day — it is one or the other, by date, and the code calls that unconditional. There is no notion anywhere of “these minutes were banked and those minutes are payable”.
Making that split real means changing how overtime pay is worked out, on a path that has been live for two weeks and that we deliberately kept simple because it moves money off a payslip. It is the right thing to want — it is just not a layout change, and we are not going to slip it in behind one.

So we propose building it in two steps. First the screen, with both sections, the claimed hours and the double-claim checks against overtime — useful on its own, and it stops anyone claiming the same evening twice. Then the payroll split as its own piece of work, with its own plan, because it deserves to be looked at on its own.

Two smaller things worth knowing

You already have the configuration switch. Section B is off by default per company, with the thresholds, break deduction and grace period you would expect. Nothing appears for anyone until it is turned on.

What gets stored per row becomes finer. Today we record a credit per date. You are asking for the claimed start and end, the actual clock-out and the eligible minutes as well — which is what makes an approver able to check the claim, and what the payroll split would later need. That part is worth doing now even though the split comes later.
