THE SITUATION TODAY

Thank you for the correction. You are right: salary should run to the Resignation Date, the official end of employment. It should not stop at the Last Working Day.

Since the last update, a future Termination already shows the employee as Active - Serving Notice straight away, and payroll can be run before the date. But payroll stops paying at the Last Working Day. If an employee clears annual leave from 11 to 20 Nov, November pay stops on 10 Nov. The Employment tab also says "Payroll pays up to the last working day", which is wrong.

There is also a labelling problem. On the Termination event, the box called "Resignation Date" actually stores the Tender Resignation Date (the day notice was given). The real Resignation Date on the Employment tab stays empty.

WHAT WE'LL CHANGE

- Payroll pays up to the Resignation Date. For a termination that is not a resignation, such as a dismissal, it pays up to the termination's Effective Date. The Last Working Day no longer cuts salary short. The existing proration formula and pay-item rules stay exactly as they are; only the date changes.

- On the Termination event, the box now called "Resignation Date" is renamed "Tender Resignation Date". The Termination's Effective Date becomes the employee's Resignation Date.

- As soon as HR saves or edits a Termination, the Employment tab shows all three dates straight away, even when they are in the future: Tender Resignation Date, Resignation Date and Last Working Day.

- The Last Working Day can be on or before the Resignation Date. This applies to both Termination and Resignation events, so an employee can clear annual leave after their last physical day.

- The wording on the Employment tab becomes "Payroll pays up to the Resignation Date 20-Nov-2026". Leaver reminders, such as the leave balance, appear in the month of the Resignation Date.

- If HR changes any of the dates, the Employment tab and the payroll preview follow the latest saved dates. The Movement tab still shows one Termination line with its change history, not a duplicate.

- An open payroll month that was already processed shows the usual "needs reprocessing" flag for the affected employee. Closed payroll, payslips and statutory records are never changed; the usual closed-payroll notice appears instead.

WHAT YOU'LL GET

Example: Tender Resignation Date 20-Oct-2026, Resignation Date 20-Nov-2026, Last Working Day 10-Nov-2026. Basic salary and prorated allowances for November are calculated up to 20-Nov-2026. HR can enter this in October and check November payroll before the employee leaves.

GOOD TO KNOW

- Employment status timing does not change. The employee shows Active - Serving Notice until the Last Working Day and Resigned after it, and system login ends on the Last Working Day. Only pay uses the Resignation Date. If you want the status to stay Serving Notice until the Resignation Date, reject this plan and say so.

- We're not touching how anything is calculated. EPF, SOCSO, EIS and PCB follow the prorated wages exactly as they already do for leavers.

- Some employees who have already left may have a Last Working Day earlier than their Resignation Date. Where such an employee is in an open month that was already processed, that month is flagged "needs reprocessing" so HR can review it before anything changes. Nothing is changed in closed months.

- Leave encashment stays a suggested figure for HR to decide, as today.
