2Text that is too long is cut off, not wrapped

This is the main fault, and it is worse than a cosmetic one — a payslip that names a pay item only halfway is a payslip that does not say what the employee was paid for.

Today

A real payslip file, produced by the system today, shown at the size it prints. The pay item names are a test payslip chosen to be long; the dots are the document's own.

  • Three of the seven lines are cut off mid-word and finish in dots: HANDPHONE AND COMMUNICATION AL… · TRAVELLING ALLOWANCE (SITE VISITS) … · SITE SUPERVISION RESPONSIBILITY AL…
  • It happens elsewhere on the same page too — a job title printed as "Construction Project Managem…", and a long enough employee name is cut in the same way.
  • Half the box is empty. Earnings and Deductions are forced to the same height so they line up as a pair, so a short list leaves blank rows while a long name beside it runs out of room.

3The two sentences at the foot of the page

Both come off the payslip and off what your employees see.

Prorated on 31 days worked in this period. · Calculated under statutory rules version 2026.7.

One line stays: the one that says the document was produced by a system. The days worked stay on the payslip as a field in the employee panel, where they read as information rather than as a note about the calculation.

One document, two audiences

This is the same file your staff download for themselves. Everything drawn here — the month, the logo, the full pay item names, the two sentences going — lands on their payslip as well as on yours. That is almost certainly what you want; it is worth knowing before you approve it.
