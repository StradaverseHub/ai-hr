6What this picture deliberately does not promise

You listed these. The plan turns them down or leaves them out, so they are not drawn — a picture that showed them would be promising them.

  • No Draft state. There is no “Save as draft” button on the form and no Draft on the list — the four statuses drawn above are all of them. A draft nobody submits is invisible to the approver and gets forgotten.
  • No Cost Centre / Project Code field. The form has the fields drawn and no others. A cost code would collect figures that no report reads today.
  • No escalation to a department head. One name in “Goes to”, one approval, whatever the hours and whatever kind of day it is. Overtime has no second approval level to escalate into. Instead, rest-day, public-holiday and long plans are flagged to your superior so they are impossible to miss.
  • No approval deadline, reminder or nudge. Three messages are promised and drawn: your superior when a plan arrives, and you when it is approved or rejected. No reminder to a slow approver, and no reminder to you on the overtime date itself.
  • No “Request changes” button for the approver. Approve or reject, as everywhere else in the system. A rejection comes back with their remark, and you can file again.
  • No minimum notice period. Tomorrow is where the form opens, not a rule about how far ahead you must be.
  • One plan per date, not per time window. The refusal drawn above triggers on the date. Because only one plan can stand for a day, overlapping hours within a day cannot arise — but there is no clash check between two time ranges, because there can never be two.
  • The approver's own screen is not redrawn. Overtime already has its own section in their approvals inbox. It gains the kind of day and a marker on long plans; no new screen is built for them.
