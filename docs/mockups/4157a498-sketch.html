A row that fails imports nothing. If any line of an employee's month fails, that whole employee-month is held back rather than going in half-complete — a month missing one of its deductions would quietly understate that person's year-end statement.
The rest still goes in. The 950 good lines are not blocked by the 20 bad ones. Fix the failures, upload the corrected file, and only the missing months are added.
The last row is the important one. A month that already has payroll here is reported back to you and left exactly as it is. It is never written over. Why, and what we would build instead, is in the plan.

Four things we have deliberately left in words rather than drawn

There is no overwrite button in this sketch, on purpose. These figures do not sit beside your payroll — for those months they become it, and they are added into the monthly EPF, SOCSO, EIS and tax submissions and into each employee's year-end statement. Loading a month that already has payroll would add to what is there, not take its place. And a loaded month is locked, so there is no way back.
The settings behind two of the columns. A company code field already exists on the company screen (Company Label) — today most companies have it blank, so it just needs filling in. There is no short code on a payroll cycle yet, which is where ME goes, so we add one. Fill both in once and those two columns are genuinely validated rather than accepted on trust.
Nothing is ever calculated for you. Whatever amount your file states is the amount that is stored. We do not work out EPF, SOCSO, EIS or tax for a month you are loading — those were paid by your old system and the point of loading them is to keep what actually happened. That is also why a month with no contribution lines at all is stopped rather than guessed at.
