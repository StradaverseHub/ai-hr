THE SITUATION TODAY

Today every employee's monthly PCB is worked out by the system from one of five Income Tax Resident choices: Resident, Non-Resident, REP, Knowledge Worker or C-Suites. HR has no way to say "for this person, deduct exactly RM450 a month" or "tax this person at 25% flat".

When an employee has an arrangement that doesn't fit the standard formula, HR has to work around the system instead of recording the decision in it.

WHAT WE'LL CHANGE

- Two new choices in the Income Tax Resident list on the employee's Bank & Statutory, Income Tax (PCB) section: "Custom - Fixed Tax Amount (RM)" and "Custom - Tax Rate (%)". They sit below the five existing choices (see the sketch).

- Fixed Tax Amount: when chosen, a box appears for the monthly PCB amount in RM. Payroll deducts exactly that amount. No PCB formula, tax table, relief, TP1, TP3 or rate is applied, and nothing recalculates or adjusts it.

- Tax Rate: when chosen, a box appears for the percentage. Payroll works out PCB exactly the way it does for Non-Resident today (all taxable pay for the month, including bonus, times the rate, no reliefs), but uses your percentage instead of 30%. Example: RM3,000 at 30% gives RM900.

- You can't save without a valid number: the amount must be more than RM0, the rate must be more than 0% and no more than 100%, both to two decimal places. If a value is somehow missing, payroll stops for that one employee with a clear message telling HR where to fix it. It never quietly falls back to the standard formula.

- The payroll's PCB detail screen and the payslip show the PCB amount as normal. The PCB detail also explains where it came from: "Custom fixed amount set by HR", or the pay figure times the custom rate.

- The PCB file for LHDN, the EA form and the other year-end reports carry the custom PCB amount just like any other PCB amount.

- Every change is recorded: who changed it, when, and the old and new option and number.

WHAT YOU'LL GET

HR gets a per-employee manual PCB control for exceptional cases, with the decision recorded on the employee's record and in every payroll that uses it. Everyone else carries on exactly as today.

GOOD TO KNOW

- One difference from your request: we've put the two custom options under Income Tax Resident instead of adding "Custom (Manual Entry)" to Tax Category. Income Tax Resident is the setting that decides which formula runs, and it is set separately for each employer. Tax Category describes the person's family situation (single, married and so on), is shared across all their employers and feeds reliefs and forms. Keeping it untouched means one fewer step for HR, a custom setting at one company never leaks to another, and switching the employee back to standard is one click with nothing to re-enter. If you'd rather have it exactly as written, reject this plan and say so.

- Nothing changes for employees on the five existing choices. The standard PCB formula, the Non-Resident formula, reliefs, exemptions, TP1 and TP3 are not touched, and we'll prove that by checking existing results come out identical.

- When it takes effect: the next payroll you process or reprocess after saving. Closed payrolls are never changed, and each payroll keeps the exact option and number it used.

- The fixed amount is deducted in full in every month the employee is paid, including part months and bonus months. If an employee is paid more than once in the same month, the fixed amount is deducted once for the month in total, not once per run. If the amount is more than the employee's pay that month, payroll shows a warning for HR to review; it does not change the amount.

- As with Non-Resident today, zakat paid through salary is not offset against a custom PCB amount.

- Who can change it: the same HR administrators who can change Income Tax Resident for that employer today. Employees can see their own setting but cannot change or request it.

- In this round the custom options are set on the employee's record (and on the payroll's tax set-up screen, which uses the same control). The Create New Employee screen and the Excel import will get them as a follow-up; until then, create the employee and then set the custom option on their record.

- HR remains responsible for the amount or rate entered. LHDN normally expects PCB to follow its schedule, so this is best kept for cases where you have a clear basis for the figure. The system records who set it and when.
