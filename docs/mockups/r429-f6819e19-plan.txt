WHAT YOU ASKED FOR

A simpler Shift page about how a shift works (name, label, start, end, full day hours, cross midnight, half day, breaks), with Working Days and the General section moved to the Schedule Group. Breaks shown straight away as rows with the duration worked out, flexible minutes, one way to set each break, and proper checks.

THE SITUATION TODAY

1. Working days already live on the Schedule Group too. When a shift's Schedule Group sets a working week, that week is the one used and the Mon–Sun row on the Shift page is greyed out. When the group sets none (the Standard group is left without one on purpose), the shift's own row is still the one used. When we last checked, 2 of 8 shifts worked this way.

2. The General section holds: the Schedule Group the shift belongs to (which also decides its lateness, early-out and overtime rules), normal-hours rounding, three boxes saved for reference that nothing uses (Day type, Full-day leave, Full-day overtime), the overtime time windows, and the shift premium.

3. Breaks: rows appear only after Add Break, otherwise there is one "Break (min)" box. There are two ways to make a break flexible: a whole-shift switch with its own minutes, and a per-row Fixed tick (the reverse of Flexible) with its own minutes. A row with its end before its start, or half filled in, is silently left out on save. Overlapping or duplicate breaks are not checked.

4. Something to know: the boxes called "Shift Start Time" and "Shift Time Out" are not the shift's hours (those are "Time in" and "Time out"). They are the earliest clock-in and latest clock-out, and nothing checks clockings against them yet.

WHAT WE'LL CHANGE

The Shift page, in three sections

1. Shift Information: Shift Name, Shift Label (the short code, e.g. AM01), colour, description, category. Below, for reading only: the shift's Schedule Group and its working days, with a link to change them there.

2. Working Time: Shift type, Start Time, End Time, Cross Midnight (shown automatically when the end is at or before the start), Full Day Working Hours (worked out, can be changed) with normal-hours rounding beside it, 1st Half End and 2nd Half Start, the daily cut-off, and the clock-in window renamed "Earliest clock-in" and "Latest clock-out", marked for reference.

3. Breaks: an always-visible table of Break, Start, End, Duration (worked out), Flexible, Paid and Delete. A new shift opens with a blank Break 1 row (a blank row saves nothing). + Add Break adds a row.

4. Ticking Flexible opens a minutes box, filled in with the break's length, which you can lower; those minutes come off every worked day, whenever the break is taken. Not ticked, the break counts as today: only the minutes the clocking covers between its start and end.

5. The overtime time windows and shift premium move to a closed panel at the bottom, "Overtime and pay for this shift". They stay with the shift: each shift has its own values and payroll reads them, so moving them to a Schedule Group would change pay.

6. Gone: the Mon–Sun row, the General heading, the Schedule Group dropdown (changed only on the Schedule Group page under Shifts allowed, which already does this), the whole-shift flexible switch, the Fixed tick and the three reference-only boxes.

Checks on the breaks (said on the row, Save refused until fixed, and checked again when saved)

7. Start must be before End, unless the shift crosses midnight; a night shift's break may now run past midnight, for example 23:45 to 00:15.

8. A break cannot be zero minutes long, cannot overlap another break (two identical breaks count as overlapping), and must fall inside the shift. A half-filled row is reported, not dropped. A flexible break needs its minutes, from 1 up to its length.

The Schedule Group page

9. Working Days (Mon–Sun) moves up to sit straight after General.

10. It lists every shift in the group with the working days it really uses today: the group's week, or the shift's own saved week where the group sets none.

11. While you edit the week, it shows which shifts' days would change (e.g. "Saturday Off → Half") and warns that overtime changes from the day you save. Nothing changes until Save.

Elsewhere

12. The Shift list shows each shift's Schedule Group and the working days actually in force.

13. When a shift with no working days is chosen as someone's fixed shift, the refusal says to put it in a Schedule Group that has a working week.

WHAT HAPPENS TO WHAT IS ALREADY SAVED

No attendance, overtime or pay figure changes because of this release.

1. Each shift's own saved working days stay as they are and are still used wherever its Schedule Group sets no week. To change them, give the Schedule Group a working week.

2. Which Schedule Group each shift belongs to does not change.

3. Break times, Paid ticks and per-break flexible minutes stay. A break ticked flexible with no minutes has always counted like a fixed one; it now shows as not flexible and counts the same.

4. The whole-shift flexible allowance: in September no shift used it. We check again before release; any shift that does has the same minutes moved onto its break rows, so every day comes out identical. One where that cannot be done exactly (e.g. no break times) is left as it is, and its Breaks section says so.

5. Shifts with a break length but no break times keep it, shown under the table as "Break without set times: 60 min, taken off any day of 6 hours or more", as today. Entering break times replaces it, and the break is then counted by its times.

6. The three reference-only boxes keep their values; they are no longer shown, and nothing used them.

7. A shift whose saved breaks overlap calculates as today but cannot be saved until fixed. We count these before release.

Only when someone saves from now on: a new shift starts with no break (today it starts with 60 minutes), and a night shift may have a break across midnight.

WHAT IS NOT DONE

1. Maximum rest days, maximum off days, maximum hours per week, rotation (frequency and week start) and duty roster controls. None is stored today, and a box with nothing behind it would mislead. Each needs its own request saying what should happen when a limit is reached, or how a rotation moves people on.

2. Clockings are still not checked against the earliest clock-in and latest clock-out.

3. A new shift has no working days until it is added to a Schedule Group that has a working week. The Standard group deliberately sets none, because every shift outside a group falls back on it.

4. No weekly hours total on the Schedule Group: its shifts can have different hours, so one figure would be wrong for some.
