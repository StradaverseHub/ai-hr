WHAT YOU TOLD US

Borang 8A stopped you with "Employee D — IC", although Employee D is not Malaysian. You asked that the identity number follow the employee's nationality: the IC number for a Malaysian, the passport number for anyone else, and that a non-Malaysian with a passport is never blocked for having no IC. You are right: Borang 8A asked every employee for an IC number.

WHAT WE'LL CHANGE

- The identity number on Borang 8A follows the employee's status, the same status payroll already uses for contributions:
  - Malaysian citizen: the IC (MyKad) number.
  - Permanent resident: the MyPR number.
  - Non-citizen: the passport number.

- A non-citizen with a passport number is no longer stopped for having no IC. Their passport number prints in the identity column.

- If the number that applies is genuinely missing, the message names that number, for example "Passport number" for a non-citizen, so you know exactly what to fill in.

- The same rule applies to the EIS (SIP) contribution schedule, which lists the same people.

WHAT YOU'LL GET

Borang 8A goes through for staff of every nationality, with the right identity number on each line.

GOOD TO KNOW

- The status comes from the employee's EPF citizenship status on their statutory record (citizen, permanent resident or non-citizen). The same field decides their EPF rate. If someone's status is wrong there, their contribution rate is wrong as well, so it is the one place to correct.

- We hold a permanent resident's MyPR number in the same box as an IC number, because the two cards use the same 12-digit format.

- If a citizen has no IC number on file but has a non-Malaysian nationality and a passport number, the passport number is used rather than blocking the form.

- The column keeps the heading printed on the agency's own form (No. Kad Pengenalan), even on a line that carries a passport number, because that is how the form itself is laid out.

- The PERKESO upload file is a separate matter. Its one identity box takes a 12-digit IC number or a 12-character SOCSO foreign worker number. Employee D has no IC, and the SOCSO number on their record is shorter than 12 characters, so the upload file names them and leaves them out rather than writing something PERKESO may reject. If your PERKESO officer tells you how a shorter foreign worker number should be entered, tell us and we will add it. The printed Borang 8A itself is not held back.

- No SOCSO or EIS amounts change.
