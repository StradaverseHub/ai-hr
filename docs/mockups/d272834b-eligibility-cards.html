3. Why the obvious fix is wrong, and what we will do instead

When the system grants leave it deliberately treats a missing fact as qualifying. That is not sloppiness — it was written after a real incident. 55 of your 75 active employees have no gender recorded, and eight of your leave types are ticked for both men and women. Reading a blank as “does not qualify” would have stripped Annual Leave, Medical Leave and six others from those 55 people.

The distinction that settles it. Your eligibility screen offers exactly two genders to tick. So a type ticked for both is not a restriction at all — it is every option there is. A type ticked for one is a real restriction. We will treat them differently:
Leave typeTickedGender not recorded →
Annual, Medical, Compassionate, Study, Exam, Graduation, Caregiver, Disastermale + femalegranted — nothing is being restricted
Maternity Leavefemale onlywithheld, and listed for you
Paternity Leavemale onlywithheld, and listed for you
So the incident that rule was written to prevent cannot happen, and a gendered leave stops being handed to someone we cannot place.
We will not delete 5,390 days behind your back. Withholding stops it happening again; it does not tidy up what is already there. We will give you the list of 55 so you can fill the gender in — most of them probably should keep it — and only reverse what is still wrong once you say so.

The rest of your request

“Statutory entitlements are never blocked” will mean what you said it should. For someone who IS eligible, the statutory minimum cannot be cut by other settings. It will not make a leave type appear for someone who fails the rules.

It applies to every rule, not just gender — marital status, minimum service and probation behave the same way, through the one shared check.

Nothing changes for an eligible employee. No entitlement, balance or approved leave of anyone who qualifies is touched.
