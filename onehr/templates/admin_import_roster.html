How it works

  • New Employee ID → the person is created with the Manager from their row (required for a new joiner) and the HOD if it's given (a BG admin can assign it later on Leaders).
  • Existing Employee ID → their details are refreshed; the same record is kept, so their past reports and history stay intact. A returning leaver is reactivated.
  • Manager / HOD columns take the manager's / HOD's Employee ID. Required for new joiners; optional when updating an existing person (left blank keeps their current link). That is also what makes someone a line manager or an HOD: they need no column of their own.
  • BU CEO of (one or more units of their business group, separated by ";"), BG CEO of (a business group) and Group Chief of (a calibration group's name) set their seats. Blank leaves a seat as it is; NONE takes it away. The check lists every seat that changes, and who it is taken from. A business group's admin can set BU CEO and BG CEO in their group, a unit's admin no seat, and only the super admin Group Chief of.

Columns (header names are flexible / case-insensitive): Employee ID, Employee Name, Business Group, Company, Job Grade, Job Title, Department, Joined Date, Confirmation Date, Year(s) of Service, Employment Type, Work Email, Cost Center, Work Location, Manager, HOD, BU CEO of, BG CEO of, Group Chief of. People already in OneHR keep their passwords. Joined Date is their first day in the group (Group People's Original Hire Date), not the date of a move between companies.

Quality first, then all-or-nothing. Choosing a file changes nothing: OneHR checks every row first. A row that can't be imported (no Employee ID, Name or Business Group, outside your data, an admin account, a new joiner with no Manager or HOD, a unit that isn't in Org structure) stops the file until you fix it. Things that are probably a mistake (a manager who has left, a reporting loop, a department spelled two ways, a shared or missing email, a date or grade OneHR can't read) are listed for you to look at before you go on.
